# Year-one financials

Build an assumption-driven structure. Do not fill cells with invented dollars.

## Structure

| Block | What to list |
|-------|----------------|
| Money in | How a sale happens, what is sold, and which driver moves revenue (customers, seats, jobs, retainers) |
| Money out | Cost groups: making the offer, finding buyers, tools, people, and compliance |
| Cash timing | When cash arrives versus when it leaves (deposit, invoice lag, prepaid, monthly) |
| Solvency | What must stay true for the year to close with cash still in the account |

Use labels, units, and formulas. Example: `monthly revenue = paying customers × average payment`. Leave the factors blank or marked until confirmed.

A figure the founder or an advisor confirmed is a fact; write the date next to it. Every other figure is an assumption or `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`.

## Clarifying questions

Ask these before treating any number as real.

1. What has a customer already paid, or what have they said they would pay?
2. Which costs are already being spent, and which are still a plan?
3. How many days sit between a yes and cash in the account?
4. How many paying customers, seats, or jobs does year one need for cash-out to stay below cash-in?
5. Who confirms the spreadsheet: the founder, an accountant, or another advisor?

Stop at the structure and these questions when the answers are missing. Do not estimate a market or a bank balance to make the section look finished.
