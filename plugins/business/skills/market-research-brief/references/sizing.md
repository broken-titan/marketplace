# Demand, segments, and size

Write these blocks in order. Leave a figure blank or marked when the source is stale or missing.

## Demand

State who already pays to solve this, what they buy today, and what would make them switch. A demand sentence needs a dated source or `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`.

## Segments

Name the buyer groups the offer can reach in the stated geography. Each segment needs a rule the founder can test (who buys, who pays, who is out). Mark `[DECISION NEEDED]` when the founder has not chosen which segments are in.

## TAM, SAM, SOM

Define TAM as everyone who could buy, SAM as the slice the offer can reach now in the stated geography, and SOM as the share the founder can win in the period under study. Each of those three needs a current URL and a retrieval date. Recheck fast-moving inputs (pricing pages, store ranks, census extracts, ad-library spend) on the day of the draft.

Write the unit next to each figure (buyers, seats, jobs, dollars). Show the cut from TAM to SAM to SOM as a filter the source supports. If the cut is a working guess, label it an assumption.

## Trends

List dated movements that change demand in this geography: regulation, price floors, channel shifts, or substitute adoption. Each trend needs a URL and a retrieval date, or `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`.

Ask the founder for a source they already trust before you search. Keep a figure only when you can point to the page you used.
