# Don't

## Words

leverage, utilize, synergy, delve, unpack, realm, landscape (as metaphor),
underscore, pivotal, robust, seamless, holistic, comprehensive (as puff),
foster, empower, unlock, unleash, showcase, boast, nestled, vibrant,
meticulous, intricate, myriad, plethora, testament, journey (as metaphor),
transform, revolutionize, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, facilitate,
commence, aforementioned, align (as corporate), bandwidth (for time),
stakeholder (when you mean people), tapestry, elevate, enhance,
game-changing, load-bearing, supercharge, paradigm, nuanced,
fundamentally, essentially, commendable, paramount, invaluable,
groundbreaking, multifaceted, enduring, interplay, garner, bolster,
thereby, consequently, constitutes.

Scope, do not blanket-ban: `precise` for a measurement is fine; empty
`precisely` / `exactly` / `specifically` is not. `mechanism`, `wiring`,
and `dynamics` are fine as the engineering term; not as a metaphor for
an argument.

## Sentence habits

- The dunk family: `X is not Y` / `it's X, not Y` / `Not X, but Y` /
  `is more than X, it is Y` / `rather than simply` / `rather than merely` /
  `rather than relying` / `not simply` / `less like X and more like Y`.
- "Isn't just X, it's Y."
- Flagging importance with no number: `this matters`, `why X matters`.
- The couplet: a short rule, then a second sentence that explains, restates,
  threatens a future, or scores the mistake.
- If the first sentence still needs a second sentence to make sense, make
  the first sentence specific.
- A semicolon may join two closely related independent clauses that are both
  actions or both conditions; it must not smuggle a gloss.
- The three-adjective pile ("clear, concise, and compelling").
- "X plays a role in shaping Y."
- A closer made of three short fragments. Write a complete sentence.
- Restating the heading as the first sentence under it.
- A skill YAML `description` that is a slogan, a keyword dump, or a Hard
  rule copied into the trigger. Write when to use the skill.
- Saying the same fact twice.
- Decorative ensures / highlights / supports / reflects when a concrete
  verb would do.
- Intensifiers with no number: significantly, effectively, crucial, vital,
  essential, increasingly, precisely, exactly, specifically.
- Superlatives with no comparison set: the most powerful, perhaps the most,
  the most popular, single most, arguably the most.
- Evaluative puff with no metric: dependable, meaningful, thoughtful.
- Punchy fragments for drama ("Not a detail. A design decision.").
- Building to a turn of phrase. State the claim, then stop.
- False-balance closers: "it depends," "while this may vary,"
  "many people feel."
- Copula dodging: "serves as," "stands as," "functions as" when you mean "is."
- Replacing a banned construction with a new slogan ("fails the bar" for
  "it's X, not Y"). Say the concrete problem.
- A slogan under the title. If the first Hard rule already says it, go from
  the title to Files or Rules.
- Sentence-initial Furthermore / Additionally / Ultimately this.
- A leftover closer of about 3-4 words that states a spare fact; put the
  fact in the sentence that needed it, or leave it out.

## Also don't

- Short sentences that only reinforce and add no information.
- A colon as a drumroll into a slogan. Titles and labels may use a colon.
- Over-explanation. The reader is not an idiot. Avoid "Two things and only these two."
- Stating what you are not doing, or decisions not made.
- Enthusiasm inflation ("great question," "exciting," "amazing").
- Hedged recommendations. Pick one and say why.
- Blending an elevated in-fiction voice into rules text or vice versa.
- Over-formatting. No bold. No heavy markdown. Plain headings and short paragraphs.
- Reproducing typos. The voice is direct, not sloppy.
- Unspaced em dashes. Asides use parentheses or a spaced en dash ( – ).
